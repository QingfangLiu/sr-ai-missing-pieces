Fig. 4. Aligning outcomes across measures and follow-up times in a systematic review. A. Outcome measures with different names may represent the same construct. In a Cochrane review of exercise for knee osteoarthritis [89], pain was assessed using the Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Osteoarthritis Index (WOMAC) pain subscale [90, 91], the Knee Injury and Osteoarthritis Outcome Score (KOOS) pain subscale [92, 93], and a visual analogue scale (VAS) [94, 95]. These measures can be mapped to the common outcome of pain. Conversely, the WOMAC stiffness subscale, WOMAC physical function subscale, and WOMAC total score represent distinct outcomes despite sharing the WOMAC acronym. Mapping measures to a common construct does not by itself establish that their results can be pooled. B. Reviewers must also decide whether results obtained at different follow-up times belong in the same synthesis. A Cochrane review comparing gastrostomy with nasogastric tube feeding pooled mortality irrespective of follow-up time [96], whereas a Cochrane review of exercise-based cardiac rehabilitation for coronary heart disease synthesized all-cause mortality separately within short-term (6-12 months), medium-term (>12-36 months), and long-term (>36 months) follow-up windows [97]. The input boxes show selected reported study follow-up durations: 20 days, 28 days, six weeks, three months, and six months in the tube-feeding review; and 6, 12, 18, 24, 60, and 120 months in the cardiac-rehabilitation review (five and ten years are expressed in months). Arrows show how these durations enter one pooled mortality synthesis or separate time-specific syntheses. The displayed durations are selected examples, not an exhaustive representation of either review. These examples illustrate review-specific grouping decisions rather than a universally preferred approach. The diagrams show grouping across studies; they do not imply treating repeated measurements from the same study as independent observations. Reference numbers follow the supplied manuscript.
