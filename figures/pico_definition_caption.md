**Figure 3. The critical step of establishing review-level PICO-S.** **A**, Reviewers translate a review question into review-level population, intervention, comparator, outcome, and study-design definitions, which determine study eligibility. This formulation step is typically supplied by humans in current LLM-based systematic-review workflows. **B**, The lumper–splitter decision determines whether related compounds—illustrated by fluoxetine, paroxetine, and sertraline—are grouped as one intervention class (newer-generation antidepressants) and pooled or analyzed separately to obtain compound-level estimates. **C**, Eligibility rules for co-interventions should reflect clinical context. When a co-intervention is standard, as with smoking-cessation behavioral support delivered with pharmacotherapy, excluding combination studies may omit most relevant evidence. When a co-intervention is optional, as with psychotherapy added to antidepressant medication, restricting eligibility to studies of the intervention alone may yield a cleaner effect estimate. These examples illustrate why reviewers should pre-specify co-intervention rules based on real-world clinical practice. Indigo and terracotta distinguish the lumper and splitter approaches in panel B, while two intensities of teal distinguish the standard and optional co-intervention scenarios in panel C. Colors denote alternative decision structures rather than correct and incorrect choices.
